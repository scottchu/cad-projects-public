# Initial listener questions

Task: S-Q0. Role: private presentation questioner. Input: the firsthand anecdote supplied in the task; no deck or repository design evidence reviewed.

These are prompts for the writer to resolve from the story and available visuals first. The presenter does not need to answer all six. “A million times” is understood as frustration and hyperbole, not an iteration count.

1. **What were you trying to make the XT60 rail mechanism do, in one ordinary action I can picture?**
   - Why it matters: I need a small mental picture of the intended result before I can care that it kept failing.
   - Answer could come from: a source visual with one plain-language caption; user recollection if the intended action remains unclear.

2. **Can you show me one attempt that looked promising, and the exact moment you realized it would not work?**
   - Why it matters: One recognizable failure makes the repeated frustration tangible without a catalog of versions.
   - Answer could come from: a source visual of an actual attempt plus the user's recollection of discovering its problem. Do not infer the discovery from geometry alone.

3. **When you say you relied fully on AI, what did you actually do next each time an attempt failed?**
   - Why it matters: A concrete exchange or action lets me recognize the loop you were stuck in, rather than hearing a general lesson about AI.
   - Answer could come from: user recollection or an existing recorded exchange. Authoring can shorten it, but should not invent dialogue.

4. **What was the first relationship the simulation made you see that you had been missing?**
   - Why it matters: This is the turning point. A visible “when this moves, that has to…” connection would let the audience share the discovery.
   - Answer could come from: a simulation view showing the specific relationship, grounded by user recollection or source evidence. Avoid choosing a constraint merely because it sounds plausible.

5. **What did that discovery feel like—relief, annoyance, embarrassment, excitement, or something else?**
   - Why it matters: The emotional change connects the frustrating attempts to the fun of finally understanding something.
   - Answer could come from: user recollection only. These examples are prompts, not attributed feelings.

6. **What was the very next thing you did differently with AI after seeing that relationship?**
   - Why it matters: A small changed action gives the story an ending even if the physical mechanism is still unfinished.
   - Answer could come from: user recollection, a subsequent recorded request, or a demonstrated next step. Keep any claim about a working print separate from the learning moment.

Priority: resolve questions 1, 2, 4, and 6 in the story or visible demonstration. Questions 3 and 5 can add voice and feeling when the speaker has a real example. Optional technical curiosity belongs in speaker notes or Q&A, not in the main story.
