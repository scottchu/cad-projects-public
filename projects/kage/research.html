Axial height becomes a radial shadow.

The clock does not use long flat hands. Three hidden rings rotate parallel to the wall. One small upright fin rises from each ring edge toward a single on-axis LED, and perspective projection stretches that fin into a readable wall shadow.

Correct physical stack

Orientation rule: each fin stays at one radius. Its broad face spans tangential × axial directions. A radial-axial plate would show the LED only its thin edge and is rejected.

Point-source math

R(r,z,S) = rS / (S-z)
z(r,R,S) = S(1-r/R)
Lshadow = Rtip - Rstart
Wprojected = wS / (S-z)

Wall is z=0; positive z points toward the viewer. The LED is at S=60 mm. Carrier pitch is 4.75 mm. All three wall shadows begin at r=48 mm, just inside the projected central mask r=50 mm.

What to print first

  1. One fake quartz/backplate with a stepped central arbor.
  2. Three manually rotated hidden rings.
  3. One 1.6 mm and one 2.0 mm upright-fin coupon before the full set.
  4. An adjustable external LED holder and removable matte glare card.

No gears, final enclosure, or separate halo circuit yet. Prove shadow length, sharpness, fin stiffness, alignment and temperature first.

Wired source, not phone flash

Use one small warm-white wide-angle emitter on a metal thermal path, aimed toward the wall. A Cree XQ-E class emitter at 350 mA is the point-source starting class; a large COB, diffuser, LED strip or RGB G4 bulb makes the source too broad for sharp shadows.

Electrical/thermal boundary: use a compatible 350 mA constant-current driver, regulated supply, insulated wiring and a real aluminum heatsink. Printed plastic is only a fixture. Disconnect and discharge power before rewiring; never hot-plug the LED output.
