Current prototype: `P13 four-plate / three-rod equal-travel mechanism`

## 1. Goal

Hold and form the **two insulated XT60 pigtail wires only** while soldering and
cooling. The XT60 plastic body must not be the clamped load. The required wire
path is a fixed 90-degree bend from the ESC solder pads toward the XT60.

The prototype must remain:

- parametric and easy to retune from named inputs;
- driven by one rotary input;
- built around three purchased 304 stainless rods, each `Ø4 x 50 mm`;
- open enough to load, solder, cool, and remove the completed pigtail;
- mechanically simple, with no base, frame holder, decorative handle, or angle
  adjustment in P13.

## 2. Current mechanism

- Four circular plates: two fixed guide plates and two synchronized cam plates.
- Three rods move the same scalar distance along three different straight guide
  directions.
- The rods are asymmetric by function: forming, supporting, and locking.
- `0°` is OPEN.
- `22°` is first geometric tangency with the nominal wire envelope.
- `22°..27°` adds a final closing stage.
- `27°` is LOCKED.
- The locked wire centreline is exactly 90 degrees; adjustable bend angle is out
  of scope.

## 3. Frozen purchased hardware and print assumptions

- Rods: `3 x 304 stainless steel, Ø4.00 x 50.00 mm`, uncut.
- Intended prototype material: PETG.
- Nozzle used for the fit coupons: `0.4 mm`.
- Use `uv` for Python environments and commands.

silently replace other functional bores with this value: each bore's role must
be checked first.

### Cap/throat coupon

The earlier cap set was too tight and its tiny `1/2/3` markings did not print
reliably. The latest three cap-throat specimens were all judged usable; the two
left specimens felt nearly identical. There is not yet enough physical evidence
to declare one exact throat dimension the universal winner.

## 5. Current wire-clamping change

The previous motion stopped at theoretical tangency and could appear too loose.
The current parametric source adds:

- `wire_preload = 0.25 mm`;
- `contact_stroke = 3.7642135623730946 mm`;
- `common_stroke = 4.014213562373095 mm`;
- nominal wire OD `4.30 mm`;
- rod radius `2.00 mm`;
- wire radius `2.15 mm`;
- tangent centre offset `4.15 mm`;
- locked centre offset `3.90 mm`.

`wire_preload` is accepted by the source only over `0.00..0.40 mm`.

This is a **geometric compression proxy**, not a force calculation. CAD does not
prove silicone-wire force, insulation safety, PETG stiffness/creep, thermal
behaviour, or cooled springback.

## 9. Assembly and physical test sequence

1. Print the production 3MF in PETG.
2. Assemble the four plates, D shaft, keeper, three permanent left caps, three
   rods, and three removable right caps.
3. Before soldering, sweep the mechanism through the complete `0°..27°` travel.
4. Confirm OPEN permits loading the two wires and completed XT60 removal.
5. Confirm approximately `22°` produces first contact rather than visible wire
   compression.
6. Confirm LOCKED holds both wires firmly without cutting/pinching insulation or
   loading/deforming the XT60 plastic body.
7. Confirm rods and caps can be serviced/disassembled without damage.
8. Only after the dry test succeeds, test soldering and cooling.
9. Measure and report the actual wire OD with calipers.
10. After cooling, measure the resulting bend angle and note springback.

Report these observations:

- `0.25 mm` preload: too loose / correct / too tight;
- any rod/slot binding through the full motion;
- cap retention and removal effort;
- PETG flex, cracking, creep, or heat softening;
- insulation marking, flattening, or damage;
- soldering-iron access;
- whether the soldered XT60 can be removed normally;
- final cooled angle and springback.
